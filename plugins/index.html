Official plugins

The plugins in mosaikit-plugins. Each is a .mskp package the Administrator installs from the Plugins page; the platform installs only packages signed with a trusted key. Versions are those of the repository today: 31 plugins. Select a picture to see its screenshots.

Applications

Documents 0.5.3

Files and folders the whole team can reach, with the same sharing as everything else in Mosaikit. Upload single files or whole folders, preview images, text and PDF in the browser, download a folder as a zip, unpack a zip into a tree of folders and give a file or folder a public link.

Typical uses

  • A shared archive of contracts, offers and minutes where each folder is shared with its own group
  • Sending a large deliverable to a customer with a public link that you can switch off
  • Keeping the receipts, scans and photos of a project next to the tasks that use them

Notes 0.9.1

Personal notes written in Markdown (bold, lists, check lists with a progress bar) that look like coloured post-its. Pin the ones that matter, archive the rest, share a note with colleagues and groups, export it as Markdown, and edit the same note live with other people.

Typical uses

  • Meeting minutes written together while the meeting is going on
  • A quick shopping or preparation list with boxes to tick
  • Ideas and drafts kept private until you decide to share them

real-time

Tasks 0.6.0

A to-do manager with a quick-add line, a list grouped by due date, a board and a week view you can drag tasks across. Give each task a priority, labels, a project, a check list and a repetition; assign it by sharing it and the person finds it in their own list.

Typical uses

  • Planning the week of a small team and seeing who has what
  • Recurring duties such as monthly reports or weekly checks that renew themselves
  • Breaking a job into a check list and following its progress

Contacts 0.5.0

A shared address book with cards or an A to Z list, tags, birthdays, normalised and validated emails and phone numbers. Import and export vCard and CSV, and find duplicates to merge.

Typical uses

  • A company address book every colleague sees and nobody keeps in a private spreadsheet
  • Moving contacts in from a phone or a mailing list and cleaning the doubles
  • Remembering the birthdays of customers and suppliers

Chat 0.1.0

Conversations in channels that are shared like any other item: whoever can read a channel follows it, whoever can write talks. Messages arrive at once with who is online and who is typing, Markdown, replies, reactions, editing and deleting, and a button that starts a video call in the channel.

Typical uses

  • A channel per project or customer, open to exactly the people involved
  • A quick question to the team without opening a mail thread
  • Jumping from a discussion into a call with one click

real-time

Meet 0.1.0

Video meeting rooms that open inside the page, with camera, microphone and screen sharing, shared like any item so you decide who can enter. The room shows who has it open; the call runs on the meeting service and no video goes through Mosaikit.

Typical uses

  • A standing daily meeting in a room everybody knows
  • A customer call from a room shared with the customer team only
  • A workshop with screen sharing for a distributed team

Announcements 0.1.1

A notice board for the people you share it with: publish a notice with a title, a text in Markdown, a level (information, important or urgent) and the period it is valid. Urgent and pinned notices stay at the top, expired ones move to Past, and a notice can ask readers to confirm with one click, so the author sees who has read it and when.

Typical uses

  • Office closures, new procedures and safety notices that everybody must see
  • "Read and acknowledged" evidence for a policy or an instruction
  • A pinned notice board for a team, a department or a whole organisation

Bookmarks 0.1.0

A shared collection of web links: each bookmark has a title, an address, a note and tags. Search across all of them, filter by tag, mark favourites, and open or copy a link with one click. A bookmark can be shared with people and groups like any other item, and nothing is fetched from the sites you save.

Typical uses

  • The links a team keeps coming back to: dashboards, wikis, vendor portals
  • A reading list or a research collection built together and sorted by tags
  • Onboarding links for new colleagues, shared with a whole group at once

Snippets 0.1.0

A shared collection of reusable texts and pieces of code: give each one a title, a kind (plain text, shell, SQL, JSON, JavaScript, Python, Rust or Markdown) and tags, then find it by search, kind or tag and copy it to the clipboard in one click. A snippet is an item of the platform, so you can share it with people and groups like any other.

Typical uses

  • Standard replies and signatures that the whole support team pastes every day
  • Shell commands, SQL queries and configuration fragments that nobody wants to retype
  • Boilerplate for documents, e-mails and code that a team keeps in one place

Polls 0.2.0

A quick question to a group: write the question and two to twelve options, choose single or multiple choice, set an optional closing date and decide whether the voters are named. Everyone the poll is shared with votes with one click (read access is enough) and can change their vote while it is open; the results are bars that move live while others vote, shown always, after the vote or once the poll is closed. With a public link, people outside the organisation vote too: once for each browser, without ever seeing who voted, and the owner sees them counted as visitors.

Typical uses

  • Choosing a date, a place or a lunch for the team in one minute
  • Collecting opinions on a proposal, with anonymous votes, or a vote with people outside the organisation
  • A decision with a deadline, closed by hand or on its closing date

real-time

Surveys 0.1.1

A series of questions answered by the people a survey is shared with: one answer, several answers, free text or a scale from 1 to 5. Each person answers once, and the author reads the results per question (a bar per option, the average of a scale, the text answers) and exports them to CSV. In quiz mode the answers are scored, with a pass mark, and each person sees their score right after submitting.

Typical uses

  • Customer satisfaction or team climate surveys with results per question
  • Onboarding quizzes with a pass mark and an instant score
  • A quick poll of a department, a course or an event, exported to a spreadsheet

Approvals 0.1.1

A request that one or more people must approve or reject, with the history of who decided what and when. Choose the approvers and the rule (one approval is enough, everybody must approve, or one after the other), share the request with them, and each one decides once, with a comment. The state is always worked out from the decisions, so nobody can change the past.

Typical uses

  • Purchase requests that a manager and the finance office must sign off
  • Holiday and remote-work requests with a clear answer and a reason
  • Sign-off of a document by several people before it goes out

real-time

Dashboard 0.1.0

One page you arrange from widgets that show what matters to you across the apps: your open and overdue tasks, notices waiting for your confirmation, polls to vote, requests to decide, your recent notes. Apps add their widgets by themselves, every widget shows only your own data, and a board can be shared. Keep several boards, one columns layout each, and see them refresh every minute.

Typical uses

  • A morning page with what is due today and what waits for your decision
  • A team board shared with the people who follow the same work
  • One place to start from instead of opening each app in turn

Forms 0.1.0

A form builder: add fields (text, long text, one or several choices, number, date, email), mark the required ones and share the form. Colleagues fill it in inside the platform, and a public link lets anyone answer with no account, while the visitor never sees the answers of others. The answers land in a table with the counts per option, ready to export to CSV; a closing date and a cap on the answers keep the form under control.

Typical uses

  • Event registrations with a menu choice and a headcount
  • Requests of citizens or customers sent through a public link
  • Order forms and sign-up sheets collected in one table

Projects 0.1.1

Give a set of tasks a plan. A project has the columns of a board, milestones with dates and sprints (time boxes), and a progress bar that counts how many of its tasks are done. The tasks stay in the Tasks plugin and keep their own sharing: you arrange only the ones you may already see and change there.

Typical uses

  • A product release with a beta date, a launch date and two-week sprints
  • A construction site or an event with milestones, owners and deadlines
  • A campaign whose tasks move from "To do" to "Done" on a shared board

Calendar 0.1.1

The agenda of the team. Write events with a day, a time and a repeat, invite people of the organisation and see everything by month, week or day. The invitees answer yes, maybe or no, and the open tasks with a due date appear next to the events, so deadlines and meetings are in one place. Times are local times as written, with no time zone conversion.

Typical uses

  • The team agenda with meetings, invitations and answers everybody can see
  • Shared holidays, absences and recurring events such as the weekly standup
  • Deadlines from Tasks next to the meetings of the same week

Whiteboard 0.1.1

A shared free-form board for the people you share it with: sticky notes, rectangles, ellipses, text, arrows and freehand drawing on an endless canvas. Everyone who has the board open sees the changes and the pointers of the others live, readers follow along without being able to change anything, and the board is saved in the item, so it is there the next time.

Typical uses

  • Brainstorming and retrospectives with sticky notes, during a call
  • Flows, diagrams and quick sketches drawn together
  • A visual plan of a workshop that people can come back to and export as PNG or SVG

real-time

Maps 0.6.1

A catalogue of GeoLibre maps: each map is a project that you open, edit and share. The configuration is kept in the project file, so a map travels with its data and layers.

Typical uses

  • Keeping the maps of a territory or a site in one place with the right people allowed to edit
  • Sharing a read-only map with a colleague who only needs to look
  • Versioning the maps of a survey campaign

Sites 0.8.0

Publishes a static site from a zip archive, or shows an existing site by its web address, inside the page. Sites are shared like any item, can have a public link, a thumbnail and a card view, and a language change follows the host.

Typical uses

  • Publishing a documentation site or a landing page prepared elsewhere
  • Embedding an external dashboard or intranet page next to your tools
  • Handing a customer a private preview of a site with a link

Extensions

Small additions to an application.

Note review 0.2.1

An extension for Notes: mark a note as reviewed, with who did it and when, and see the history of the reviews of each note.

Typical uses

  • Procedures that must be read and approved by a second person
  • A trace of who checked the minutes before they were sent
  • A simple quality step on documents written as notes

Layouts

How the interface is arranged: pick one per tenant.

Project 1.0.1

A layout of the interface: the global navigation sits at the left and the sidebar of the area next to it. Every tenant picks its layout.

Typical uses

  • Tenants with many apps and many pages per app
  • Teams used to a tool with a persistent side navigation
  • Wide screens where the left rail costs nothing

Suite 1.0.1

A layout of the interface: apps open from a launcher grid and the pages of the area sit in a bar under the band, like an office suite.

Typical uses

  • Organisations that think in apps, not in menus
  • People who switch often between a few big apps
  • A clean screen with the content up front

Tabs 1.0.1

A layout of the interface: apps and pages are tabs under the band and a menu holds the pages of an app, like a browser.

Typical uses

  • People who keep several things open and move between them
  • Tenants with a few apps used all day
  • A familiar look for users who live in the browser

Workspace 1.0.1

A layout of the interface: a top bar with a wide search and a drawer that opens and closes, to give the content the whole screen.

Typical uses

  • Search-first work: finding things matters more than browsing
  • Small screens and laptops
  • Content-heavy pages such as documents and maps
