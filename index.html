Everything an enterprise application needs, already there

The kernel knows nothing about your domain, and that is the point: it does the platform work once, correctly, for every plugin and every product.

Multi-tenant from day one

Every customer, department or project is a tenant, with its own people, groups, policies, quotas and brand. Isolation is enforced in the database, not in the application code.

Three roles, real permissions

Administrator for the platform, Tenant Manager for a tenant, User for the work. Resources are shared with people and groups at three levels (read, write, manage), with inheritance on folders.

Single sign-on with OpenID Connect

A tenant signs in with local accounts or with its own identity provider, such as Keycloak. Administrators always keep a local account with a second factor (TOTP).

Public links

Share a resource outside the tenant with a link that is read only, expires, can have a password and counts the visits.

KPIs and an activity log

Per tenant for the Tenant Manager and across tenants for the Administrator: metadata and aggregates, never the contents.

Quotas and usage

Limits for every tenant on what the kernel can measure, shown to the people who reach them, so a tenant never surprises you.

Notifications, search, trash

The bell, the global search, the trash with restore and the help of the shell work for every plugin without a line of code.

Your language, your look

The interface is in English with Italian available, and a language is just a plugin. Light and dark themes, and the colours of the tenant.

One brand, yours

Name, logo, favicon, colours, texts of the sign-in page and the desktop icon come from one brand.toml. Your users see your product, not ours.

See it

A sign-in page that looks like your product

Choose the tenant, sign in with a local account or with the identity provider of the tenant. The page takes the theme and the brand of the platform, and of the tenant as soon as it is known.

Sharing that every plugin gets for free

People, groups, three levels, inheritance and public links belong to the kernel. A plugin declares a resource and its users can share it, without a line of permission code.

Plugins that feel part of the product

A plugin brings its logic, its pages and its data. Its interface runs in an isolated frame, yet receives the language, the theme and the colours of the tenant, so it looks native.

Operate many tenants from one place

The Administrator creates tenants, follows their members, items and space, chooses their sign-in and plugins and their quotas, and watches the aggregates of the whole platform.

A catalogue of signed plugins

The Administrator makes plugins available; the Tenant Manager turns them on for the people of the tenant and decides their permissions. Every package is signed, and a blocklist can stop a bad one everywhere.

Why developers build on it

Write only your domain

No tenants, no login, no permissions, no admin screens, no multi-tenant database design. A plugin is a manifest, some logic and a UI.

Safe by construction

Logic runs as WebAssembly with limited fuel and memory; files, network and clock only through the host. SQL is limited to the tables of the plugin. The UI lives on its own origin with a strict content security policy.

The same rules on PostgreSQL and SQLite

Row-level security on PostgreSQL; query rewriting, triggers and an authorizer on SQLite. Develop on your laptop, deploy on a server.

Server, desktop or cluster

A Windows desktop app, a Docker image with Compose files and a Kubernetes release: one command each, from the compiled platform.

A stable, versioned API

Plugins declare the platform versions they need. The plugin API has compatibility rules and a conformance check, so an upgrade of the platform does not break your plugin silently.

No platform source needed

The Dev Kit has the tools, the SDK, the UI API, three commented examples and the documents. You never clone the kernel.

Try it: a plugin in ten minutes, a product in a day

Download the Dev Kit for your system, put its bin/ folder on your PATH, and choose.

Plugin

Add a feature to Mosaikit

mosaikit-plugin new my-plugin
cd my-plugin
mosaikit-plugin dev

A running server with test users, your plugin loaded and reloaded at every change. Start from a new plugin or copy a commented example (--example todo).

Getting started

Product

Ship your own application

mosaikit-product new acme-maps
cd acme-maps
mosaikit-product dev --base 1.0.2
mosaikit-product build docker --base 1.0.2 --build

Your name, brand, plugins and defaults on the compiled platform. The Base is downloaded and checked for you; you only add Docker to ship.
